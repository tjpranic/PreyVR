# The player body does not turn with the player — investigation, 11 October 2026

Branch `playability-improvements`, from `stereo-both-eyes` at `15780df`. Static and code
analysis only: nothing here has been run in the game or a headset. Reported by the wearer
(and listed as open in [STEREO-DUAL-SUBMISSION-2026-10-10.md](STEREO-DUAL-SUBMISSION-2026-10-10.md)):
turn around physically or snap-turn 180 degrees, look down, and you see the character's back.

## Cause

Four yaws, all about +Y:

| | Meaning | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| **G** | native player yaw: what the body mesh, capsule and arms rig face | engine |
| **R** | reference yaw (`room.yaw` / `gReferenceYaw`), tracking space | mod |
| **H** | head yaw, tracking space | headset |
| **P = G − R** | world yaw of the tracking space | derived each frame |

The rendered view is `P + H` ([HeadTrackingHook.cpp:416-459](../src/dll/HeadTrackingHook.cpp#L416-L459)).
The body faces `G = P + R`. Nothing keeps R near H:

- **Physical turning** changes only H.
- **Snap turn** (the default, 45°) changes only R ([HeadTrackingHook.cpp:678-695](../src/dll/HeadTrackingHook.cpp#L678-L695)).
  The documentation says so: "The native body yaw is not directly rewritten" ([COMFORT-CONTROLS-2026-09-19.md](COMFORT-CONTROLS-2026-09-19.md)).
- **Recenter** sets R = H once.

So the body faces wherever the player faced at the last recenter, and every physical turn or
snap since then leaves it behind. Smooth turn (`move.snap 0`) is the exception: it posts
`xi_thumbrx` and the engine turns G itself
([MoveLane.cpp:427-455](../src/dll/MoveLane.cpp#L427-L455)), so the body turns with the stick,
though still not with the head.

A torso estimate already exists (`armpose::TorsoYaw`: 35° deadzone, relaxes at 0.6/s). The arm
solver uses it for the shoulders but it has never been applied to the body. That is why, from
[ARM-POSE-2026-10-06.md](ARM-POSE-2026-10-06.md): "Turned 90° physically, the game's own torso
mesh intrudes at the bottom of the view."

## A native seam: the engine's own view-rotation setter

No code in this repo writes G. RTTI is stripped, so the names below are by shape, not by symbol.
All of it is pinned by `tools/re/verify_view_rotation.py`, which passes 11/11 on the supported
Steam hash. See the [address registry](ADDRESS_REGISTRY.md) "View-rotation static additions".

- **ArkPlayer vtable slot 52** (`+0x1A0`, RVA `0x1584CC0`): a setter with the
  `IActor::SetViewRotation(const Quat&)` shape. It does three things:
  - copies q to ArkPlayer `+0x13F0`, which is `ArkPlayerCamera::m_rotation`, the quaternion
    `UpdateView` reads and save games store as `"rotation"`;
  - calls the player entity's virtual `+0x118` with `(q, 0)`, the `IEntity::SetRotation` slot in
    CryEngine's layout;
  - hands q to a component at `+0x1468`, which **keeps its own copy**.

  Slot 53 is the matching getter.
- An entity rotation event (`0x1580AE4`) copies the entity rotation back into `+0x13F0`. The
  engine keeps the view rotation and entity rotation in step.

**Use the setter, not a field write.** Writing `+0x13F0` alone would leave the `+0x1468`
copy and the entity stale.

**Other routes, and why not:**
- **Posting look input** (`xi_thumbrx`, `maxis_x`) gives a rate, not an angle. It is scaled by an
  unmeasured turn speed and by the GLOO multiplier, so the view could not be compensated exactly.
- **Hiding the body mesh** removes the symptom and the body with it. Keep it only as a fallback.

## Recommended design: the reference yaw is the torso yaw

**Invariant: R = T**, where T is the torso yaw in tracking space. Then the body faces
`P + T`: the torso's real direction in the world.

**Per frame, on the game thread:**

1. Update T from the head (one shared estimator; see below).
2. Compute `d = wrap(T − R)`. If d is negligible, stop.
3. Call the setter with `level(Rz(d) · GetViewRotation())`, then set `R += d` in both copies
   (`gRoomReference` and `gReferenceYaw`).

G and R move by the same d, so **P does not change**. Aim, hands, interaction, the wrist and
panels do not move.

**Why this covers every turn source:**
- **Snap turn needs no change.** It still changes R and pivots the origin about the head. On the
  next update the follower sees `T − R = step` and turns the body by it: a snap turns view and
  body together.
- **Recenter and the runtime rebase** work the same way.
- **Smooth turn and any native rotation** (mouse, scripts, level load) compose too. The follower
  writes a relative delta on top of the current native rotation, so it never cancels them; they
  change P as they do today.

**Relative delta, not an absolute target.** An absolute write of `G = P + T` would fight every
native rotation the follower did not cause.

**No generation bump for follower updates.** About 25 consumers reset on a reference-generation
change, including IK calibration (with 90 settle frames), the two-hand hold, gestures, panel
re-fits and the render contract. The generation exists to announce a change in P, and the
follower never changes P. Snap and recenter keep their bumps.

**Level the native pitch.** Write a yaw-only quaternion. Native pitch bends the torso (mouse pitch
did, [HEAD_TRACKING_SCOPE.md](HEAD_TRACKING_SCOPE.md)), and a save made while looking down
would otherwise carry that bend into VR. Nothing in the VR pipeline reads native pitch: the
reticle and interaction rays are mod-owned.

**One torso estimator.** Today there are three:
- the arm solver's, in `AnimIkTakeover`; it updates only while ADIK runs;
- the holsters' and medkit's (`BodyEquipment.h`: 45° deadband, 90°/s);
- none at all for the body.

The visible body, the shoulders and the hip and chest slots should all agree. Hoist one owner,
updated every frame, read by all three. Recenter resets it to H, so the body faces the new
forward. The defaults stay as today (35° and 0.6/s), so physically turning the head alone does
not swing the body, and a sustained turn brings it round within seconds.

### Call site, and the one timing hazard

The setter must run **before the frame's arm IK and before `UpdateView`**:

- **If it runs after IK:** the hands were placed in the old model frame, then rendered with the
  new entity rotation. They are off by d for one frame. That is about 0.2° per frame during
  relaxation, but 45° on a snap, which would show as a flash.
- **The `CSystem::Render` hook** (where snap turn runs today) is after both IK and the view, so it
  is the wrong place.
- **The candidate is the entry of `ArkPlayer::Update`** (`0x1584FE0`, reproduced live under R-021).
- **Fallback:** the prologue of the existing `UpdateView` hook, before the original. That covers
  the view but may not precede IK.

The code comments say the render hook and the gameplay `UpdateView` both run on the main
thread. That is not measured.

### What each consumer does under this design

From a survey of every yaw-coupled site in `src/`.

| Consumer | Frame it uses | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| View, aim (`aim.bodyyaw 1`), shot ray, interaction, scene query, psi, grenades, physical, wrist, debug overlay | P | Unchanged |
| IK world goals; IK model space from the native ADIK `location` | P; G | Correct by construction: goals re-expressed in the turned rig. Shoulders now sit on the visible body |
| Head-relative movement (`H − R`) | becomes `H − T` | Still correct: stick relative to the body, which is now the torso. Run the follower before `UpdateMoveLane` so the post matches the frame's G |
| `move.headrelative 0` | G | Changes meaning from snap-relative to torso-relative, which is arguably the point |
| Heavy carry (`CarryLane.cpp:734-740`, `HandCarry.cpp:292-296`) | native lerp target (G) plus a P-based shift | **Needs review**: the object would swing with the torso |
| Holster and medkit slots | own torso estimate | Switch to the shared one |
| Legacy hand rig and weapon attachment, `aim.bodyyaw 0` | `cam − R` | Shift by d. Inactive or diagnostic in VR mode. Leave them, or retire them |

## Implementation order

1. **A pure module** in `src/common` with tests:
   - the follower step: d, the yaw-only composition and the wrap;
   - invariants: P unchanged, R = T after a step, a snap absorbed in one step, recenter reset,
     native rotations preserved, non-finite input refused.
   - Then the shared torso estimator, moved out of `AnimIkTakeover`.
2. **The native lane** with gates:
   - player present, VR active, gameplay (not menus or the cinematic gate), camera mode 0;
   - stop and report on a prologue mismatch.

   Toggles: `body.follow 0|1`, with `body.torso <deadzoneDeg> <relax‰>` replacing `arm.torso`,
   and a COMFORT page option in the free slot.

   Counters:
   - applied and refused;
   - last d;
   - the measured error `|yaw(G at UpdateView) − (P + T)|`;
   - the readback after each set.
3. **Live acceptance**, as hypothesis, control, variable and decision rule:
   - **Seam.** With `body.follow 0` as the control, one call of a new diagnostic (proposed:
     `body.turn 30`, which calls the setter once) should move
     the native yaw by exactly 30° (within 0.1°) at the next `UpdateView`, with no snap-back over
     the next 300 frames and an unchanged P. Any snap-back means the `+0x1468` component or the
     look integration owns the angle: stop.
   - **Order.** Log a per-frame sequence of ArkPlayer::Update entry, the ADIK callback,
     `UpdateView` and the render hook, with thread ids. The chosen call site must precede ADIK
     and `UpdateView` in every sampled frame.
   - **Wearer.** Look down after a physical 180° and after four snaps: the body faces the view.
     Turn the head ±30°: the body stays put. Hold 60°: it follows within seconds. No hand flash
     on a snap. Movement still follows the head. A two-handed hold survives a snap.
4. **Carry and holsters** on the shared torso, then retest heavy carry while turning.

## Not in scope here

- **Room-scale translation.** The capsule does not follow the head when the player walks around
  the room, so the body is left behind by distance as well as by angle. That was agreed out of
  scope in [ARM-POSE-2026-10-06.md](ARM-POSE-2026-10-06.md). This design leaves room for it: the
  same game-thread lane is where a capsule follower would live.
- **A torso from the controllers** (hands' average yaw) rather than the head alone. It may read
  better when looking sideways while holding a weapon forward. Try it once the head-only
  version is in a headset.
