# VR arm pose: the arm comes from the player's shoulder

Branch `jordi/polishing`, on top of [the hand pose](HAND-POSE-2026-10-06.md). Measured in Prey with
the headless OpenXR mock (1280 × 1440 per eye, Quest 3 field of view) when written; in every
headset build since, and corrected on 2026-10-19 (below). `arm.mode 0` restores the native arm at
runtime for A/B; `pose.mode 0` turns off both this and the hand pose. Where the shoulders sit on
the player's body, and the body itself, are in [BODY-FOLLOW.md](BODY-FOLLOW.md).

## What was wrong (measured, mock)

With the hands on the controllers, the game still hung each arm from the shoulder its **weapon
animation** authored, and that shoulder never moved with the player:

| Weapon | Right shoulder vs the player's | Left shoulder |
|---|---|---|
| Wrench | 4 cm | 4 cm |
| GLOO | 7 cm back | 14 cm forward (the idle blades the torso ~28°) |
| Pistol | 12 cm back, 10 cm inwards | 6 cm forward |

The idle animations rotate the clavicles, not the spine. Consequences: with the arm extended the hand
stopped short of the controller (pistol 10.6 cm, GLOO 5.8 cm), and elbows were 15-21 cm from where
the player's are (pistol, right arm), sometimes chicken-winged. The rig's arm is also short and
oddly proportioned (upper arm 23.3 cm, forearm 28.4 cm; an adult's is ~30 / ~26). See
`docs/evidence/arm-pose-2026-10-06/native-gloo-views.png` and `before-pistol-views.png` (side and
top views; local only, like all evidence PNGs, which `.gitignore` keeps out of the repo).

## Engine facts this rests on (measured with throwaway writes)

- Moving the upper-arm joint in the **absolute** pose before the ADIK pass does nothing useful: the
  pass recomputes the animated shoulder from the clavicle.
- Writes to the **absolute** pose **after** the pass are what the engine skins: an elbow moved there
  deforms the sleeve, and a hand moved there carries the held weapon with it (it hangs from
  `*_handProp_jnt`, a child of the hand).

## What changed

1. **The player's torso** (`armpose::TorsoYaw`): yaw-only, from the HMD, robust looking straight up or
   down. It follows the head with a 35° deadzone (turning the head alone does not turn the shoulders)
   and relaxes towards it at 0.6/s, so a physical body turn is followed within seconds. It is now
   the shared torso the body turns to ([BODY-FOLLOW.md](BODY-FOLLOW.md#the-four-yaws)).
2. **The player's shoulder**: the glenohumeral centre of an adult relative to the centre eye in that
   frame (−9 cm forward, ±18.5 cm out, −23 cm up: the anthropometry Dishonored VR uses), reached by
   **rotating the rig's clavicle** about its root, stretched by at most 15%.
3. **Reach** is measured from that shoulder with the player's arm (28.5 + 27.5 cm, may stretch 10%;
   exact up to 92%), not from the animated shoulder. The hand-pose palm offset is unchanged.
4. **After the pass** the arm is rewritten: clavicle turned, upper arm at the shoulder, the elbow,
   forearm, and the hand on its goal (it is already there unless the native solve fell short) with
   its fingers and prop joint. Twist and muscle helpers ride their parent, lengthened along the bone
   with it.
5. **The elbow** (`armpose::ChooseElbowPole`) is a MAP estimate over the circle of elbows that reach
   the wrist, using the player's **real** hand from the controller's grip pose (not the drawn hand,
   which a weapon turns to its barrel): wrist flexion ~N(0, 45°), deviation ~N(−8°, 12°) (a hand round
   a controller sits a little ulnar), a steep wall at the range of motion (flexion 75°, extension 65°,
   radial 20°, ulnar 35°), the elbow's swivel ~N(0, 25°) about its natural direction (down, out, a
   little back), and the previous frame's elbow as a prior so it never flips between near-equal
   choices. So the forearm follows the hand's twist, a hand that says nothing leaves the elbow
   hanging, and a hand that would need an impossible wrist raises the elbow as a person's would.

## Commands

`arm.report`, and `arm.mode 0|1`, `arm.shoulder <fwd> <out> <up>` (mm), `arm.len <upper> <fore>` (mm),
`arm.stretch <clavicle‰> <arm‰>`, `arm.knee <‰>`, `arm.prior <flexSd> <devRest> <devSd> <swivelSd>`,
`arm.rom <flex> <ext> <radial> <ulnar>`, `arm.hold <sd> <tauMs>`, `arm.torso <deadzoneDeg> <relax‰>`.
`pose.marks` now also gives the clavicle, shoulder, elbow, the character's neck and head, the wrist
goals and the solver's shoulder targets, as drawable segments and as `J`/`P` data lines.

## Verification

- `tests/ArmPoseTests.cpp` (CTest `arm_pose`): torso frame and yaw (wrap-around, any pitch),
  deadzone/relax, shoulder target and clavicle reach, two-bone solve and stretch, wrist angle signs,
  elbow choice (thumb up, palm down, continuity, no information). All 59 project tests pass.
- **Known arms.** `build/jordi-mock/truth.py` builds an arm (shoulder, elbow, wrist, a hand with a
  given wrist bend and forearm twist), turns it into the aim pose such a player's controller reports,
  and feeds the mock; the drawn arm is then compared with the arm that produced the input. Nine poses
  (relaxed, ready, reach, aim high, hand at the face, out to the side, across the body, palm up,
  overhead), both arms, GLOO / pistol / wrench / Boltcaster: shoulders on target, **palms 0.0 cm**,
  **elbows 0.2-6.7 cm** (mean ~2.5). The pistol before / after, same inputs:

  | Pistol, right arm | `arm.mode 0` | `arm.mode 1` |
  |---|---|---|
  | shoulder | 16.2 cm | 0.0 |
  | elbow | 15.5-21.1 cm | 0.6-5.8 cm |
  | palm, arm extended | 9.2 cm short | 0.0 |

- **Offline** (`build/jordi-mock/elbowsim.py`, the solver ported to Python, 400 random arms per
  population): natural arms, mean elbow error 4.5 cm, p90 9.7 (the anatomical direction alone: 6.9 /
  15.1); controllers held at the edge of the wrist's range, 4.6 / 8.7; with the hand frame rotated by
  N(0, 15°) at random, 5.4 / 11.3.
- **Stability** (`stability.txt`): the wrist rolled through 180° in 10° steps moves the elbow at most
  1.2 cm per step; a 1.5° controller tremor moves it 0.11 cm rms.
- Two-handed GLOO acquires, holds, steers and releases; body turns of 60° and 90° keep the
  shoulders within 2 cm; losing a controller drops that arm back to the native solve and picks it up
  again; equip and inventory transitions run through it.

## Limits

- **The shoulder offsets are an average adult's;** `arm.shoulder` adjusts them. The segment
  lengths follow the Arm Length option (2026-10-19, below).
- **The body was the game's when this was written:** it did not follow the player. Leaning 15 cm
  forward was absorbed by the clavicle; 15 cm sideways left the shoulders 9-10 cm off (the arm
  stayed connected, elbows 3-7 cm); a physical crouch left them 4 cm high; turned 90° physically,
  the game's own torso mesh intruded at the bottom of the view. The body now turns with the
  player, follows them across the room and is posed under their head, and the shoulders ride its
  chest ([BODY-FOLLOW.md](BODY-FOLLOW.md#the-shoulders)).
- On the mock, poses with the controller pointed down from the belly (aim −35°) raise the elbow: with
  the mock's estimated grip-from-aim (40°) those inputs need an impossible wrist otherwise. A headset
  reports the real grip, which is all the elbow uses.
- The drawn weapon hand is the weapon's, so its wrist can look bent relative to the forearm, which
  follows the player's real hand.

## 2026-10-19: the player's arm length, and the shoulders from the neck

The wearer reported the arms bending inward, as if there were too little room between shoulder
and wrist. Of the two causes ([BODY-FOLLOW.md](BODY-FOLLOW.md#the-shoulders)), the shoulder hung
from the eyes was the body's: it now comes from the neck (`armpose::ShoulderFromHead`, laid out
from `armpose::LevelEye`, the eye as it would be with the head level and facing the torso), and
since 2026-10-25 it rides the drawn chest. The other was the arm's:

- **The arm was always 56 cm.** A player with shorter arms got a bent elbow even looking ahead.
  **Arm Length** (Hands tab, `arm_length`, 40-70 cm, default 56; `arm.length <cm>`) sets both
  segments in the 28.5 : 27.5 split.
  - `arm.report` gives `reachCm` and `reachMaxCm` per arm: the player's wrist from the shoulder,
    now and at its longest since the last `arm.*` command.
  - Hold the arms out straight between two reports, and `reachMaxCm` is the length to set.

The mock never showed either: its known arms used exactly these shoulder offsets and segment
lengths, with the head level.

Tests (`arm_pose`):
- Head pitch and yaw about the neck leave the shoulder still, where the eye anchor dropped
  8 cm looking down 45 degrees.
- A lean moves the shoulder.
- A 50 cm straight arm draws bent with the default length and straight with its own.
