Branch `custom`, 2026-10-15. The radial wheel and the reticle are both parts of Prey's
gameplay HUD movie (`DanielleHUD`). Today they are drawn on the head-locked HUD panel,
2 m away. The plan, agreed with the user:

1. A shared way to lift one named part out of the HUD movie onto a texture of its own

2. The wheel on its own panel, anchored where it opens like a book, at the HUD panel's
   distance and size ([below](#step-2-the-wheel-held-where-it-opens)). **Done; offline
   tests only, awaiting the headset.**
3. The reticle, with the interaction prompt and quick loot, placed in the world where the
   aim ray hits ([below](#step-3-the-reticle-at-the-depth-it-marks)). **Done; offline tests

## Step 1: lifting a part out (`HudLayer`)

The wrist's status display already did this for its meters. It is now general:

- **Profiles.** `hud::Isolation` (`include/preyvr/NativeHudIsolation.h`) takes a `Profile`:
  the paths a replay leaves out, and the paths whose drawing proves it reached its part.
  The wrist's is `StatusProfile`, unchanged. The wheel's is `hud::Wheel.replay`: every
  other named clip left out, proved by reaching `wheel_mc`.
- **Parts.** A `hud::Part` names:
  - the subtrees lifted off the HUD's own draw;
  - its replay profile;
  - its boolean gates (the wheel's three `isOpen`s, step 2);
  - diagnostic probes;
  - numeric overrides set for its replay alone (result 3).
- **The HUD's own draw** leaves out groups of paths through `hud::Exclusion`: the wrist's
  meters (`wrist.face`) and each lifted part. Each group is left out whole or not at all.
  One that does not resolve stays in the draw without costing the others. A part that
  stayed is not replayed as well, so it is never drawn twice.
- **When a part is lifted** (`hud::PartLift`):
  - its consumer requested it within the last 200 ms;
  - and its gate reads open.
  - After the gate closes it stays lifted while the replay still draws, at most 1 s, so the
    close animation plays on its own layer.
  - An unreadable gate never lifts: the part stays where Prey draws it.
  - So a closed wheel costs one boolean read per frame, and no replay.
- **The replay** runs inside the HUD's one native display callback, after its own draw and
  after the wrist's replay. It uses the same sprite filter (`replayFilter`) and the same
  transaction as the wrist: handles resolved and released within the callback, and the
  native stencil copied before the HUD's own draw. A failed transaction latches that part
  off until the XR session restarts, and it goes back to the HUD's draw.
- **API** (`HudLayer.h`): `RequestHudPart(part, active)` once per XR frame the consumer can
  show it; `TakeHudPart(part)` once per frame, returning the texture (the HUD's size, the
  part alone), whether the replay drew it, and the gate; `ResetHudParts()` at session
  teardown.
- **Diagnostics:** `hud.layer` adds
  `parts={wheel={requested= lifted= drawn= open= refused= fault=}}`. Here `lifted` and
  `drawn` count frames, and `open` is the gate as last read (1, 0, or -1 for unread or
  unreadable).

Step 1 alone changes nothing in play. The wrist and `wrist.face` now run through the
general code, with the same paths and the same order.

## Step 2: the wheel held where it opens

`XrSessionHost` requests the wheel every frame while the HUD is on its floating panel and
no game menu is up. With the HUD drawn in the world instead (HUD style 0), the wheel stays
in the HUD as before. Lifting it there would change how that mode draws the HUD.

While the wheel is lifted, it is shown on a panel of its own:
- **Where it goes.** It is placed in front of where the wearer looks when it opens, the
  way a book is (`ui::AnchorPopup`: the gaze, pitch limited to 35° up or down, no roll).
  It is held there while it is up (`ui::PopupHold`, 300 ms grace) and placed afresh when
  it next opens. A completed recenter re-places it; a tracking-space change lets it go.
- **How it looks.** The HUD panel's own fit: 2 m away and the same `ui.scale`, so it is
  the size it was on the HUD. It just doesn't follow the head.
- **When it shows.** It opens with `spiral.isOpen` and plays its close animation before it
  goes, because the part stays lifted while the replay still draws (step 1). Meanwhile
  Prey fades the rest of the HUD out and back in on the HUD panel, as it always has.
- **Layer order** (`ui::ComposeFrame`): over the HUD, under the note and text panels. Like
  them, it is left out while VR Options or a game menu is up.
- **If its swapchain is refused,** `RefuseHudPart` logs `preyvr_hud_part disabled
  part=wheel` and the wheel goes back to the HUD until the session restarts. The log
  names `wheel_swapchain` on creation.

   combined gate cannot hold the group lifted in normal play.
2. **Prey draws the panel or its prompt apart from its parent** (the more likely one). If
   so, leaving `wheel_mc` out of the HUD's draw would not leave them out. The details
   panel, its prompt (`detailsWeapon_mc.prompt_mc`) and the quick-use prompts are now also
   named in the wheel's group, so the HUD's draw leaves them out directly. If Prey's
   drawing does follow the parent, this costs nothing. If the display call of a piece
   bypasses the sprite filter entirely, naming it cannot help. The probes would then
   show it reached by neither draw.

- `samples=N probes={details_mc=… prompt_mc=… prompts_mc=…}`: on every 16th lifted frame,

- `details_mc` or `prompt_mc` reached by the HUD's draw means Prey draws the panel apart

- `gates` with the second or third count above the first means the panel outlived the

## Step 3: the reticle at the depth it marks

### What was wrong (2026-10-16)

The crosshair is part of the HUD movie, so with the HUD on its floating panel (the
default, and the wearer's setting) it was drawn on that panel, 2 m away, whatever it
pointed at. The aim lane moved it across the panel so that it lined up with the aim
(`ReticleFollow`, `HudLayerReticle`). "Reticle Fallback Distance" (10 m) and the
experimental "Scene Reticle" only chose the point along the ray it lined up with: its
direction, never its depth.

With 69 mm between the eyes, a target at 10 m and a crosshair at 2 m differ by about 1.6°
of convergence, far past what the eyes can fuse. Looking at the target doubled the
crosshair, and the other way round.

The fallback distance was a depth once. With each eye rendered in its own frame and the
HUD drawn into the game's image, the crosshair was projected per eye. Since both eyes
come from one frame (2026-10-10) and the HUD went onto its panel, the panel sets the
depth. With HUD style "in the world" both eyes get the crosshair at identical pixels, so
it reads as infinitely far.

### The change

Decided with the user: the interaction prompt and quick loot go to the crosshair's depth;
the Scene Reticle toggle is replaced by "Reticle: in the world / on the HUD" and a size;
HUD style "in the world" is left as it is.

- **The reticle part** (`hud::Reticle`): `reticle_mc` and `quadrant_C`, lifted while the
  world reticle asks, with no gate. Its replay leaves out everything else in the movie,
  `safe`'s two run-time flourishes included. It is centred for the replay alone
  (`hud::Part::overrides`):
  - `reticle_mc` and `quadrant_C` at 0, where a centred crosshair puts them;
  - `safe` at half the stage, where `Camera.HUDPos(0, 0)` puts it (the engine's HUD sway
    and shake move it from there).

  An override can now take its base from another number (`cam.m_halfStageWidth`/`Height`).
  The texture's centre is then the crosshair (verified: `Reticles.Loc`, the offsets,
  `HUDPos`).
